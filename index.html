01 — The short version

Two parallel plays — one stabilizes, one scales.

The subscription instinct is right — but on its own, the math is modest. The much bigger opportunity hiding inside this conversation is a curanderismo-rooted product line, anchored by a solstice/equinox-themed annual ritual subscription. That's the move that actually survives — and benefits from — a move to Mexico.

Recommendation

Run two parallel plays.

(1) Launch a "Sol Solace Circle" membership (~$185/mo, autopay for one 60-min session with any practitioner, small loyalty perks). Pair it with an optional Community tier for group offerings ($75/mo). This stabilizes near-term cashflow and tests demand with the existing list.

(2) Build a product line — headlined by Solsticio, an annual cyclical-practice subscription with a keepsake calendar object and seasonal refills. Add limited-edition cultural boxes (Posadas, Día de los Muertos) and a standalone apothecary line. This is where the ceiling is, and where the move-abroad fit is cleanest.

The rest of this page shows the math, alternatives, and a sequenced plan you can start this month.

03 — The real question

What are we actually trying to solve?

The brainstorm started with: "clients are losing jobs — should we lower prices via a subscription?" That's one framing. But after the finance meeting and looking at the numbers, there are really three separate problems tangled together — and they want different solutions:

1. Short-term — fill the empty rooms

Practitioners are doing ~20 combined hours/week. There's room for 2–3× that without hiring anyone new. A membership that drives regular bookings to practitioners is the cleanest fix.

2. Medium-term — retain clients in a soft economy

Some clients can't justify $200 every visit right now. They need either a lower-ticket entry point (community offerings, products) or a commitment device that makes regular care feel manageable (autopay membership). Not the same as discounting Monica's hour.

3. Long-term — what survives the move to Mexico

Any subscription or product you build now has to either (a) keep running with the existing practitioners once you're not on-site, (b) follow you to Mexico (workshops, teaching, retreats), or (c) ship from anywhere — which is where a product line shines. Michelle's bigger point — Monica's highest-value work is teaching/retreats/her own brand, not middle-management — also applies here. Don't build something that requires Monica to be in the studio.

The membership idea addresses (1) and (2) but is structurally tied to the studio. The product line addresses (2) and (3) and has the highest ceiling. Best answer: do both, sequenced.

04 — Pushing back

Concerns with the original framing

Worth saying out loud before we design around them:

Concern 1 · Pricing anchor

"$150/mo for what's normally $200" trains clients to expect a discount. Once you anchor the price down, raising it back later feels like a hike, not a return to normal. The fix: bundle added value instead of a lower headline price, and keep the discount modest (~7–10%).

Concern 2 · Cashflow vs affordability

Clients who can't afford $200 occasionally usually can't afford $185 every month either. Recurring billing is often harder on a tight budget than occasional splurges. The subscription helps the studio's cashflow predictability — but it may not actually rescue the clients losing their jobs. That's a different intervention (sliding scale awareness, products at lower price points, gift cards).

Concern 3 · Practitioner economics at $200/hr

Practitioners are on a 50/50 split of $200/hr. If the membership is $185/mo for one session, someone has to absorb the $15. Cleanest: studio absorbs it as a retention investment — practitioner still gets their full $100, studio's cut shrinks to $85 per used session. Worth deciding upfront and putting in writing with practitioners.

Concern 4 · The sliding-scale clue

You already offer sliding-scale 30-min sessions and clients aren't using them. If price was the barrier, sliding scale would be packed. It probably isn't packed because (a) people don't know about it, (b) there's stigma in asking, or (c) the format isn't what they want. A subscription has the same potential failure modes if positioning and communication aren't intentional.

Concern 5 · Move-abroad mismatch

A subscription you sell in 2026 implicitly promises continuity in 2027. If you're moving in 12–18 months, you owe members a clean answer up front: does the membership survive without Monica in the studio? Run with practitioners only? Convert to digital? Refund and wind down? Pick before you sell. This is one of the reasons the product line below matters more — it has no such tether.

05 — Three versions of a membership

Service-side options worth considering

Three structures, ordered roughly by how much they protect the premium positioning. Pick one — or layer two if you want both a "regular care" tier and a "community" tier.

Recommended

A. Sol Solace Circle — Practitioner Autopay

$185/month · with any practitioner (not Monica)

One 60-min session per month with one of the practitioners, billed automatically. Small loyalty perks — 10% off retail, free monthly community sound bath, priority booking. Session rolls over up to 2 months so missing one isn't a loss.

What it does

  • Fills practitioner hours (the underused capacity)
  • Predictable monthly revenue
  • Keeps Monica's $200 rate fully intact
  • Survives a move — practitioners can keep running it

Tradeoffs

  • Modest revenue per member; small $15 discount the studio absorbs
  • Needs practitioner buy-in on rollover policy
  • Requires clear differentiation from Monica's tier
Higher upside, more risk

B. Sanctuary Tier — Premium Bundle

$295–350/month · with Monica or senior practitioner

For top 15–25 clients. Includes one 90-min session/month with Monica or a senior practitioner, plus a quarterly add-on (limpia, sound bath, tarot reading with Tivra), plus 15% off everything else. Costs more than booking ad-hoc — the value is in priority, relationship, and curated extras, not a discount.

What it does

  • Premium positioning — exclusivity, not discount
  • Locks in deep relationships with best clients
  • High revenue per member ($3.5K–4K/yr each)

Tradeoffs

  • Demands Monica's time — opposite of "less middle-management" advice
  • Doesn't help cost-strapped clients
  • Hard to fulfill once moved unless replaced with retreats/virtual
Complement, not replacement

C. Community Circle — Group Offerings

$60–85/month · group sessions only, no 1:1

Unlimited (or 2/mo) group offerings: sound baths, breath work, herb circles, group limpia ceremonies. 8–12 per session. Lower price point creates an entry that doesn't compete with the $200 hour. Builds community and a top-of-funnel for higher-ticket products and services.

What it does

  • Captures clients who can't afford 1:1 right now
  • Margins are great — one practitioner, many people
  • Builds the community/teaching muscle Monica wants for retreats
  • Doesn't dilute any individual session price

Tradeoffs

  • Requires scheduling 2–4 group offerings/month
  • Needs a practitioner who likes leading groups
  • Different audience than 1:1 clients — marketing lift
My pick

A + C, layered. Circle ($185) for regulars who want predictable 1:1 care. Community ($75) for people who want connection at a lower price. Skip Sanctuary (B) for now — it pulls Monica deeper into hours that her teaching/retreat ambition wants her out of.

07 — The bigger opportunity

A curanderismo-rooted product line

This is the move that actually gets bigger when you leave Austin. Products ship from anywhere. Margins are higher than service. The ceiling scales with ad spend, not with Monica's hours. And the niche — authentic Mexican curanderismo as a lived practice, not an aesthetic — is genuinely uncrowded.

Why this might be the highest-value page in this doc

Service revenue is capped by Monica's hours. Product revenue is capped by demand, which scales with ad spend and email list. 100 subscribers at $245/yr is $24K. 500 is $122K. 2,000 is $490K. Sol Solace's existing ~2,000-person email list is the launch audience.

The market gap

The witchy/spiritual product space is crowded — but specific corners of it are wide open:

Crowded (skip)

Generic "witchy" monthly boxes (Goddess Provisions, House of Intuition). White sage bundles + crystal grids. Pagan/Wiccan aesthetic. Often appropriated symbols or ethically dodgy sourcing.

Wide open (Monica's lane)

Authentic Mexican curanderismo products, sold by an actual curandera. Almost nobody is doing this with integrity at scale — most curanderismo product is small-batch Etsy or in-person at botánicas. There's no obvious "the brand" in this space yet, partly because doing it right requires lineage and credibility most ecommerce founders don't have. Monica does.

What would need to be true to start

Not a launch checklist — just the few prerequisites that need to be real before this is feasible.

  • Monica's spiritual yes. The lines on what's appropriate to commercialize and what isn't — she draws them. Limpia kits and solstice ceremony objects feel commercially OK to me as an outsider; some practices clearly don't. Her call.
  • ~$8–15K initial inventory + production capital. Sourcing copal, candle production, packaging, photography, the keepsake calendar object itself. Real money up front before any revenue.
  • A sourcing relationship for copal & herbs. Ideally direct from Oaxacan growers — both for ethics and for brand story. Could start with a US supplier (Mountain Rose Herbs, etc.) for speed, switch later.
  • A photographer who gets the aesthetic. 1 day shoot covers the whole launch lineup. Budget $1.5–3K.
  • A Shopify store. Maybe 2 weeks of work for someone who's done it before. Doug's Meta background = directly applicable.
  • A FB/Meta ads strategy. Start at $30–50/day, test creative, scale what works. Conservative test budget: $1.5K to find what converts.

Honest cautions

  • The substance must outweigh the aesthetic. Beautiful packaging is necessary but not sufficient. If 60% of the budget is in written content (ceremony cards, recorded audio, instructions), you're doing it right. If it's reversed, it's an Instagram product.
  • Sourcing integrity is the brand. Sustainable Oaxacan copal (overharvesting is real), ethically grown herbs, no appropriated white sage. COGS will be 30–35%, not 15%.
  • The refill model needs a yearly theme. Year 1 "Elements," Year 2 "Ancestors," Year 3 "Plants," etc. Otherwise customers churn at year two.
  • Cross-border logistics once moved. Shipping from Mexico into US is workable (USMCA, USPS partnerships) but takes setup. Could also start fulfilling from US during the move year, then transition.
  • Don't dilute the subscription pitch. Two subscriptions max in customers' heads (Circle for services + Solsticio for products). Apothecary singles aren't a sub.

09 — A sequenced plan

What I'd actually do

A 6-month path that runs the membership and product line in parallel — but doesn't require Monica to overhaul anything at once. The goal is to test, learn, and only commit fully if numbers support it.

  1. Weeks 1–2 · Decide & align

    Pick the membership structure and lock the numbers

    Confirm Circle at $185, Community at $75, rollover policy (2 months). Align practitioners on session economics in writing. Write the one-pager for clients. Loop Deb in early so the chart of accounts supports membership revenue separately.

  2. Weeks 2–4 · Soft-launch the membership

    Founding cohort — 20–25 members from email list

    Square or Stripe recurring billing. Personal email/DM to most-engaged regulars. Frame as a founding cohort — locked pricing, gratitude for early support. Cap it. Goal: 20 paid signups in 2 weeks.

  3. Weeks 3–6 · Begin product line prep (parallel track)

    Solsticio research & sourcing

    Identify the keepsake calendar object (woodworker? potter? collaboration with a Mexican artisan?). Source copal and candle supplier. Decide on Year 1 theme. Draft ceremony content (Monica). Photographer scouting. Budget the inventory cost honestly.

  4. Weeks 6–10 · First product MVP

    Launch the apothecary singles first

    Start with the easiest SKUs — candles, copal, herb bundles, splash. Shopify store, basic product photography. Sell to the existing email list first. Use this to learn fulfillment before the big Solsticio launch. Goal: 200 orders in 6 weeks, validate operations.

  5. Weeks 8–14 · Solsticio production

    Manufacture & photograph the inaugural Year 1 boxes

    Order keepsake calendars in batch (long lead time). Produce ceremony cards, finalize Year 1 contents. Big photoshoot. Build the landing page using the mockup above as reference. Pre-orders open week 12 for fall delivery.

  6. Week 12 · Review with Deb

    Two months of membership data + apothecary data

    Compare to projections. Decide whether membership keeps, adjusts, or winds down before the move. Decide whether to add the Community tier publicly. Set production scale for Solsticio launch based on apothecary response. This is also the meeting where you discuss move-abroad timing in light of what you've learned.

  7. Late Sep · Día de los Muertos box drop

    First seasonal cultural product

    Limited run, sells through Oct 24–Nov 2 window. Test what cultural-specific product looks like vs the mass-market Solsticio.

  8. Mid Nov · Posadas Box + Solsticio launch

    Holiday season — full lineup live

    Solsticio Year 1 ships for Winter Solstice; Posadas Box ships for Dec 16. FB ads scaling against learnings from the apothecary test. The full architecture is live for end-of-year gift season.

10 — Open questions

Things we still need to figure out

Honest list of things this page can't answer without input from Monica, practitioners, or Deb:

  • Practitioner buy-in on Circle. Are they up for the rollover/scheduling implications? Do they want to be on autopay roster, or stay walk-in only?
  • Per-practitioner capacity. How many more hours/wk can each one realistically take? Sets the ceiling on Circle membership.
  • Move-abroad timing. Best guess on departure window? 12 months? 18? 24? Changes how aggressively you sell long-term commitments and how to sequence the Mexico-side fulfillment plan.
  • Monica's read on commercializing curanderismo. Which practices/objects feel right to put on a shelf? Which absolutely don't? This is the most important call on the product line and only she can make it.
  • Solsticio Year 1 theme. Elements? Ancestors? Plants? What feels grounded and lands with launch energy?
  • The keepsake calendar object. Wooden, ceramic, fabric, leather? Made by whom? This is the centerpiece of the product line's aesthetic identity.
  • Tivra's interest. Tarot membership mini-product? Inclusion in Circle as one of the practitioners? Featured in apothecary as a reading add-on at checkout?
  • Sliding-scale data. Why aren't people using the current sliding-scale offering? Quick survey of regulars could de-risk the whole launch.
  • Email list health. Of the ~2,000 subscribers, how many opened the last 3 emails? That's the realistic addressable audience for both the membership and product launches.
  • Capital plan. The product line needs $8–15K upfront. Where does that come from — savings, business cash flow, a small loan, or staged out of apothecary revenue once it's running?
