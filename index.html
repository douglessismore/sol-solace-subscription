01 — The short version

The instinct is right. The mechanism needs work.

A subscription is a smart response to a soft economy and underused rooms — but the version we first sketched (lower hourly rates billed monthly) would mostly discount existing clients without bringing in new ones, and it puts the squeeze on practitioners who are already on a 50/50 split.

Recommendation

Don't touch Monica's $200 rate. Instead, launch a practitioner-led "Sol Solace Circle" membership (~$140/mo, autopay for one 60-min session with any practitioner, small loyalty perks). Pair it with a community offerings tier (group sound baths, breath work, herb circles) that fills the lower-ticket gap without diluting your hourly rate. Test for 90 days with 20–25 founding members from the existing list. Re-evaluate before any move abroad.

The rest of this page shows the math, the alternatives we considered, and a sequenced plan you can start this month.

03 — The real question

What are we actually trying to solve?

The brainstorm started with: "clients are losing jobs — should we lower prices via a subscription?" That's one framing. But after the finance meeting and looking at the numbers, there are really three separate problems tangled together:

1. Short-term — fill the empty rooms

Practitioners are doing ~20 combined hours/week. There's room for 2–3× that without hiring anyone new. A membership that drives regular bookings to practitioners is the cleanest fix.

2. Medium-term — retain clients during a soft economy

Some clients can't justify $200 every visit right now. They need either a lower-ticket entry point (community offerings) or a commitment device that makes regular care feel manageable (autopay membership). Not the same as discounting Monica's hour.

3. Long-term — what survives the move to Mexico

Any subscription you build now has to either (a) keep running with the existing practitioners once you're not on-site, or (b) wind down cleanly. Michelle's bigger point — Monica's highest-value work is teaching/retreats/her own brand, not middle-management — also applies here. Don't build something that requires Monica to be in the studio.

The subscription idea, done right, can address (1) and (2) directly, and be designed to not block (3). Done wrong, it locks Monica into more management at exactly the time she should be doing less.

04 — Pushing back

Concerns with the original framing

Worth saying out loud before we design around them:

Concern 1 · Pricing

"$150/mo for what's normally $200" trains clients to expect a discount. Once you anchor the price down, raising it back later feels like a hike, not a return to normal. Premium positioning is hard to win back. The fix: bundle added value instead of a lower headline price.

Concern 2 · Cashflow vs affordability

Clients who can't afford $200 occasionally usually can't afford $150 every month either. Recurring billing is often harder on a tight budget than occasional splurges. The subscription helps the studio's cashflow predictability — but it may not actually rescue the clients losing their jobs. That's a different intervention (sliding scale awareness, community sessions, gift cards from friends/family).

Concern 3 · Practitioner economics

Practitioners are on a 50/50 split of $150/hr. If the subscription bills the client $150/mo, the per-session math at full usage is: client pays $150 → practitioner gets $75 → studio gets $75. That's the existing rate, not a discount. But if you discount the membership to, say, $120/mo, someone has to absorb that $30 — either the practitioner takes less per session, or the studio's cut shrinks. Worth deciding in advance.

Concern 4 · The sliding-scale clue

You already offer sliding-scale 30-min sessions and clients aren't using them. That's a signal worth paying attention to. If price was the barrier, sliding scale would be packed. It probably isn't packed because (a) people don't know about it, (b) there's stigma in asking, or (c) the format isn't what they want. A subscription has the same potential failure modes if it isn't positioned and communicated carefully.

Concern 5 · Move-abroad mismatch

A subscription you sell in 2026 implicitly promises continuity in 2027. If you're moving in 12–18 months, you owe members a clean answer up front: does the membership survive without Monica in the studio? Run with practitioners only? Convert to digital workshops? Refund and wind down? Pick before you sell.

05 — Three models

Versions that could actually fit

Three structures, ordered roughly by how much they protect the premium positioning. Pick one — or layer two if you want both a "regular care" tier and a "community" tier.

Recommended

A. Sol Solace Circle — Practitioner Autopay

$140–150/month · with any practitioner (not Monica)

One 60-min session per month with one of the practitioners, billed automatically. Small loyalty perks — 10% off retail, free monthly community sound bath, priority booking. Session rolls over up to 2 months so missing one isn't a loss.

What it does

  • Fills practitioner hours (the underused capacity)
  • Predictable monthly revenue, low risk
  • Keeps Monica's $200 rate fully intact
  • Survives the move — practitioners can keep running it

Tradeoffs

  • Modest revenue per member vs full premium tier
  • Needs practitioner buy-in on rollover policy
  • Requires clear differentiation from Monica's tier
Higher upside, more risk

B. Sanctuary Tier — Premium Bundle

$295–350/month · with Monica or senior practitioner

For your top 15–25 clients. Includes one 90-min session/month with Monica or a senior practitioner, plus a quarterly add-on (limpia, sound bath, tarot reading with Tivra), plus 15% off everything else. Costs more than booking ad-hoc — the value is in the priority, the relationship, and the curated extras, not a discount.

What it does

  • Premium positioning — exclusivity, not discount
  • Locks in deep relationships with best clients
  • High revenue per member ($3.5K–4K/yr each)

Tradeoffs

  • Demands Monica's time at the top tier — opposite of the "less middle-management" advice
  • Doesn't really help cost-strapped clients
  • Hard to fulfill once moved abroad unless you replace Monica's slot with retreats/virtual
Complement, not replacement

C. Community Circle — Group Offerings

$60–85/month · group sessions only, no 1:1

Unlimited (or 2/mo) group offerings: sound baths, breath work, herb circles, group limpia ceremonies. Capacity of 8–12 per session. Lower price point creates an entry that doesn't compete with the $200 hour. Builds community and a top-of-funnel for higher-ticket services.

What it does

  • Captures clients who genuinely can't afford 1:1 right now
  • Margins are great — one practitioner, many people
  • Builds the community/teaching muscle Monica wants for retreats
  • Doesn't dilute any individual session price

Tradeoffs

  • Requires actually scheduling 2–4 group offerings/month
  • Needs a practitioner who likes leading groups
  • Marketing lift — different audience than 1:1 clients
My pick

A + C, layered. Circle ($140) for regulars who want predictable 1:1 care. Community ($75) for people who want connection at a lower price. Skip Sanctuary (B) for now — it pulls Monica deeper into hours that her teaching/retreat ambition wants her out of.

07 — The "wellness shot" question

Lower-ticket ideas that don't degrade the hour

Monica's right that a 30-min limpia isn't a real limpia, and that her time is worth what it's worth. But "smaller spend" doesn't have to mean "smaller version of the premium thing." Here are six options that fill the gap without touching the $200 hour:

$35–55 / person

Group sound bath

60 min, 8–12 people, one practitioner. Great margin, builds community, requires no new skills.

$85–125 / person

Herb workshop

90 min, 6–10 people. Take-home tincture or salve. Hands-on, sticky, leans into the ancestral piece.

$75–125 / 30 min

Tarot reading w/ Tivra

You already have this. Promote it as a standalone offering, not just an add-on. Featured monthly.

$25–75

Retail / apothecary

Curated oils, herbs, candles, sound tools. Recurring revenue with no time cost. Great gift-card vehicle.

$50–95 / 25 min

"Reset" sessions

Foot/scalp/chair massage — clearly framed as a tune-up between full sessions, not a substitute for one.

$65–95 / person

Community limpia circle

60 min group format — ceremonial, intentional, not a watered-down 1:1. Could be quarterly, ticketed.

Bonus channel — corporate / HSA / gift cards

Three angles that are pure upside in a soft consumer economy: (1) HSA/FSA eligibility for massage therapy (where the client has a doctor's recommendation) — money already earmarked for wellness. (2) Local corporate wellness — small Austin companies looking for benefits; even one company at a $500/mo retainer is meaningful. (3) Gift cards heavily marketed in Nov/Dec — captures the "I want to give a luxury thing" budget from people other than the client themselves.

08 — A sequenced plan

What I'd actually do

A 90-day path that doesn't require Monica to overhaul anything. The goal is to test, learn, and only commit fully if the numbers support it.

  1. Weeks 1–2 · Decide & price

    Pick the structure and lock the numbers

    Confirm Circle pricing ($140 vs $150), decide on Community tier ($75 vs $85), decide on rollover policy (2 months feels right), align with practitioners on session economics, write a one-pager for clients.

  2. Weeks 2–3 · Set up the rails

    Square / Stripe recurring + member tracking

    Square supports subscriptions natively. Or use Stripe Billing if you want more control. Add a member tag/code so Michelle's bookkeeping can pull membership revenue separately from walk-in. (Worth flagging to Deb so she sets up the chart of accounts to support it from the start.)

  3. Week 3 · Soft launch

    Invite 20–25 founding members from the email list

    Personal email or DM to your most-engaged regulars. Frame it as a founding cohort — locked-in pricing, gratitude for early support. Cap it. Scarcity is real here. Goal: 20 paid signups in 2 weeks.

  4. Weeks 4–6 · Run it & listen

    First month of fulfillment

    Track: who books, who doesn't, what they ask about, what they complain about, whether practitioners are getting overwhelmed or under-booked. Schedule one group offering as a "perk" for members to see how attendance feels.

  5. Week 8 · Open it up

    Public launch to the full list

    Email + social. Use testimonials from the founding cohort. Layer in Community tier here if Circle is going well. Target another 15–20 Circle members + first 20 Community members.

  6. Week 12 · Review with Michelle / Deb

    Look at the actual numbers

    By then you'll have 2 full months of membership data in QuickBooks. Compare to projections, decide whether to keep, adjust, or wind down. This also informs the move-abroad timing — do members stick if you tell them Monica is leaving?

09 — Open questions

Things we still need to figure out

Honest list of things this page can't answer without input from Monica, the practitioners, or Michelle:

  • Practitioner buy-in. Are they up for the rollover/scheduling implications? Do they want to be on autopay roster, or stay walk-in only?
  • Actual capacity per practitioner. How many more hours/wk can each one realistically take on? This sets the ceiling on Circle membership.
  • Move timing. Best guess on departure window? 12 months? 18? 24? Changes how aggressively you should sell long-term commitments.
  • Tivra's interest. Would Tivra do a "tarot membership" mini-product, or want to be part of Circle as one of the practitioners?
  • Sliding-scale data. Why aren't people using the current sliding scale offering? Quick survey of regulars could de-risk the whole subscription launch.
  • Email list health. Of the ~2,000 subscribers, how many opened the last 3 emails? That's the realistic addressable audience for a launch.
  • Square vs Stripe. Square's subscription feature exists but is basic. If you want better tracking and member portals, Stripe + a tiny landing page is worth the small lift.
