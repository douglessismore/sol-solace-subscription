Where we actually are.

One page. No spreadsheets. Just the situation, the three real choices, and what I'm leaning toward — so we can talk about it.

The honest picture

Sol Solace has been making about the same revenue two years running. 2026 is up a little because you've been working more hours. The studio rent is the biggest single cost.

After everything is paid, what's left for our family isn't enough to also save for the move. Not failing — plateaued. And the plateau doesn't fund what's next.

Three real choices

1
Stay the course

Keep everything as it is. Studio. Practitioners. Your current hours. Nothing changes.

Move not possible
2
Reshape

A smaller space, fewer practitioners, you do less bodywork. We start selling beautiful intentional things — candles, copal, ceremony kits — the things already in your practice.

Move becomes possible
3
Release

Close the studio. You work solo from home or a small rented room. The products do a lot of the work. Brand becomes you.

Move becomes easiest

What I'm leaning toward

Path 2. Not because the numbers are biggest — path 3 actually has more financial breathing room — but because it keeps what I think you'd grieve losing.

A space. One or two practitioners. An identity beyond just you. While still making the move real.

You'd know better than me whether a smaller studio is meaningful to you, or whether it'd be holding onto something you've outgrown.

What I need from you

Not opinions on contracts or rent. Just your gut, when you have space to sit with them:

  • Which of the three feels most like the next version of you?
  • What would you grieve in path 2? In path 3?
  • Is your apprenticeship pulling you toward work that needs a studio at all?
  • Does making physical things — candles, kits, ceremony objects — feel right to put your name on?
  • How tired are you?

One more thing

You've been carrying a lot. The honest answer the numbers gave me is: not as it's currently shaped, no. But there's more than one way out — and most of them are gentler on you, not harder.
