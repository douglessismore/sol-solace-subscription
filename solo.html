The structural question

What if Monica goes solo?

The hardest path emotionally — and the cleanest one financially. Worth having on the table even if today's answer is "not yet."

What this page is, and isn't

This is not a recommendation. It's a serious look at one of the structural options on the table — the one Chelle herself raised back in February: "Monica's brand may be more valuable solo. Middle management isn't her highest level."

It deserves a real look because the numbers, when you run them, are striking. And because the alternative — staying in middle-manager mode at a thin margin — is also a choice with real costs.

Why this even comes up

A few things from the last few months point in this direction:

  • Practitioner utilization is low. ~20 hours/week combined across all of them. The 3-room studio is mostly empty. The rent ($46K/yr, 25–29% of revenue) doesn't get paid back in practitioner profit.
  • Monica's brand is the value. Per the brain doc: clinical precision + Mesoamerican lineage + apprenticing in Oaxaca. The lineage is Monica — it doesn't transfer to a practitioner roster.
  • The move-abroad goal doesn't favor a fixed studio. Anything you build that requires a 3-room Austin space gets undone the day you move. Solo Monica + products is structurally portable.
  • Doug just got laid off. The household is on one income. The fastest way to cut household risk is to cut the largest fixed cost.

The counterintuitive part

Removing the studio doesn't mean Monica has to work more. It means she has to work less for the same take-home — because no rent eats the first $46K of every year before anyone is paid. Solo Monica at 22 hrs/wk + a product line clears the family target with the biggest margin of any scenario.

Why it might make sense

What goes right

Financial

  • Rent disappears — biggest single lever
  • No practitioner cost / management overhead
  • Highest take-home of any scenario in the cashflow page
  • Lower fixed costs = lower risk if revenue dips

Strategic

  • Ecommerce ships from anywhere — move-friendly
  • Monica freed to focus on teaching, products, retreats
  • No 3-year lease tying you to Austin
  • Brand becomes about Monica, not a place

Why it's hard

What you'd give up

Operational

  • Letting go of practitioners — real people, real relationships
  • Lease termination or sublet (cost, hassle)
  • Finding a working space (home, co-op, rented room)
  • Re-routing existing client bookings

Emotional / identity

  • Sol Solace as a place — gone
  • The team — gone
  • All income concentration on Monica
  • Monica's intuitive desire to support lower-income clients harder without practitioners offering different rates

The phased version

You don't have to do it all in one move. A 12-month phased path:

  1. Now · Months 1–3

    Honest assessment per practitioner

    Who is actively building? Who is barely there? Quietly start identifying which 1–2 might be retained in a smaller model — or which 0 if going fully solo. No public moves yet.

  2. Months 3–6

    Launch products in parallel

    Apothecary singles first (validates ops), then Solsticio + cultural boxes. Decoupled from the studio question. Revenue starts to land in Q4.

  3. Month 6 · Lease decision

    Look at lease end + product revenue together

    By month 6 you'll know: (a) is product line tracking realistic or conservative? (b) when does the current lease end / what's the break cost? (c) how busy are practitioners actually getting?

  4. Months 7–12

    Pick the destination

    Full solo, hybrid (smaller studio + 1–2 practitioners), or stay-the-course based on actual data. The phased approach buys you 6 months of information before committing.

The honest read

My take

Solo Monica is the cleanest math, but it's not the only path that works. The Hybrid scenario (F) — smaller studio + 1–2 well-utilized practitioners + products — clears the target with even higher take-home AND keeps some of the practitioner leverage and team identity.

The real choice isn't "solo or current model." It's "how much of the studio is actually paying its way?"

The Solo path is on this page because (a) the numbers demand it be considered honestly and (b) it's the simplest model for the move abroad. Whether it's the right answer is Monica's call — but the question should be on the table.
