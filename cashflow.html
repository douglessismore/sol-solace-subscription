How to read this draft

This is a first-pass DIY model — directional numbers, honest best-guesses on assumptions, designed to be walked through with a financial advisor for spot-checking before committing to any direction.

The math is consistent across scenarios so they compare apples-to-apples. What needs an expert read is whether the underlying assumptions are reasonable (practitioner split, tax rate, product CAC, capacity ceilings) and whether anything obvious is missing. See What to pressure-test at the bottom.

The honest read

What the numbers say

→ Status quo doesn't fund the move. Not at 2026 trend, not at any reasonable extension.

→ Membership alone isn't enough. Useful, but capped by studio capacity. Best as a stabilizer, not a strategy.

→ Products are the unlock in every scenario that works. They're the only line item that scales without Monica's hours.

→ The studio rent is the biggest drag. Eliminating or shrinking it produces a bigger lift than any single revenue lever.

→ The two best paths (E and F) both downsize the physical studio. The question isn't whether to change the studio model; it's how much.

Year-1 reality (it ramps)

Steady-state numbers above assume products at "realistic" tier — that's roughly year 2 or year 3. Year 1 is a build year.

What year 1 likely looks like

Membership: Realistic-ish from month 2 onward. Founding cohort fills in 60 days, then growth.

Products: Conservative tier in year 1 (~$52K net). Apothecary launches first, then Solsticio for the Winter Solstice season. Real revenue starts ~Q4.

Net to family in year 1 (Scenario D layered onto current model): roughly $100–120K take-home — short of target but a real climb from $25K.

Net to family in year 2 (steady-state): the scenarios above.

Practical implication: if the move target is 2027, the product line needs to start within the next 60 days to be in real revenue by Q1 2027. If the target is 2028, there's more breathing room — but the cost is another year at thin margins.

What to pressure-test

The model above is only as good as its assumptions. Below are the variables that would meaningfully move the numbers — each one is something to walk through with a financial advisor before any direction gets locked in.

Assumptions that need a professional spot-check

  • Is the household target right? $200K total / $150K from the business is the working assumption. Could be lower if living leanly during transition. Could be higher if accounting for the move costs and time-to-rebuild abroad.
  • What's the actual practitioner split — 50/50 or 60/40? First QuickBooks pass showed practitioners at 38.7% of income (implying 60/40 to studio); the verbal agreement is 50/50. Could be a $20K+/yr difference in net.
  • Is 30% the right effective tax rate? Rough working number — refine with the CPA. Affects every take-home figure on this page.
  • Are the product line projections reasonable? "Realistic" assumes 350 Solsticio subscribers, 1,000 cultural box units, 3,500 apothecary orders/yr — all at the email-list + light-ads level. Big call to validate.

Real-world questions the model doesn't capture

  • The rent reality. Is the current lease breakable? When does it renew? What's the cheapest downsize option that keeps 1 treatment room?
  • Products capital — where does the $8–15K come from? Savings, business cash, small loan, or staged out of apothecary revenue?
  • Marketing ROI infrastructure. Already flagged — don't scale ad spend without tracking conversion. What's the minimum viable setup (pixel, attribution, dashboard) before launching products?
  • Pull up-market on positioning. Tweak brand toward luxury/discretionary income clients given the soft middle-market. Affects Circle membership pitch and product line photography/voice.
  • Practitioner-letting-go cost (if going hybrid or solo). Norms, timing, relationships. Hard to model but real. Affects Scenarios E and F.
  • Move-abroad timing window. If the target is 2027, work backwards from Q1 2027 revenue needing to be at steady-state. If 2028, more options for ramping products gradually.
  • Wellness-shot / smaller services. Lower-ticket options for clients pinched by the economy — group sound baths ($35–55), herb workshops ($85–125), Tivra's tarot, retail. Not modeled here as standalone scenarios; they layer onto any path. Worth a separate read on what fits.
