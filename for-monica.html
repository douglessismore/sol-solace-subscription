The short version

Where we actually are.

No spreadsheets on this page. Just what's true, what the choices are, and what I'm leaning toward — so you can react to it instead of getting buried in it.

The honest picture

Sol Solace has been doing about the same revenue two years in a row. 2026 is up a bit — mostly because you've been working more hours. The studio rent is the biggest single cost. Right now, after everything is paid, what's left for our family is not enough to also save for a move.

That's the story. Not failing — but plateaued. And the plateau doesn't fund the next chapter we both want.

Three real paths

Not six. Not a matrix. Three actual choices, in the language of how they'd feel to live:

1. Keep going as is

It feels safe — and we stay where we are.

The studio stays the same. The practitioners stay. You stay at the center, working roughly what you're working now. The cost of this choice is the move — it stops being possible without something else changing. Austin is the long-term home.

2. Smaller studio + a product line

It feels like rebalancing — same shape, less weight.

We move to a smaller space (one treatment room, much less rent). One or two practitioners stay — the ones who are actually busy. You step back to fewer bodywork hours and spend more time on your apprenticeship, teaching, and a small line of beautiful, intentional products (candles, copal, ceremony kits, the things that are already part of your practice). This path makes the move possible, keeps some of the studio identity, and asks you to do less of the management you don't love.

3. Solo practice + a product line

It feels like a release — and a bigger leap.

We close the studio. You work solo, from home or a small rented room when you need one. The practitioners aren't part of the model anymore. The products do a lot of the work. This path makes the move possible with the most breathing room financially, but it's the biggest change — you'd be letting go of Sol Solace as a place, and saying goodbye to the team. The brand becomes you.

What I'm leaning toward

Path 2 — the smaller studio + products. Not because the numbers are biggest (path 3 actually has more financial cushion), but because it's the version that keeps what I think you'd grieve losing — having a space, having even one or two practitioners, an identity beyond just you — while still making the move real.

But honestly, you'd know better than me whether keeping a smaller version of the studio is meaningful to you or whether it'd just be holding onto something you've outgrown.

What I need from you

Not numbers. Not opinions on practitioner contracts or rent strategy. Just your gut on a few things, when you have the space to sit with them:

  • Which of the three feels most like the next version of you?
  • What would you grieve if we did path 2? What would you grieve if we did path 3?
  • Is the apprenticeship in Oaxaca leading you toward a kind of work that needs a studio at all?
  • The product line — is making physical things (candles, kits, ceremony objects) something that feels right for you to put your name on? Or does that line feel uncomfortable?
  • How tired are you?

The bigger thing

You've been carrying a lot, and most of what's on the rest of this site is me trying to understand whether the carrying is sustainable. The honest answer the numbers gave me is: not as it's currently shaped, no. But there's more than one way out — and most of them are better for you, not worse.

None of this has to be decided this week. The point is: we have real options.
